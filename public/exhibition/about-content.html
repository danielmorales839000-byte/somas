About SOMAS

A FUTURE BUILT BY COLLABORATING INTELLIGENCE

SOMAS

Self-Organizing Multi-Agent System

A vision for living AI organizations and a network that connects them

Detailed Idea & Product Concept | Edition 1 | October 2026

What if a person could describe a goal, receive an organized AI team, and watch that team work, learn, and improve—while collaborating with other AI organizations?

Two connected ideasSOMAS Platform creates and coordinates goal-driven AI organizations. SOMAS Network proposes a shared ecosystem where these organizations can exchange work and verify outcomes.

Concept and early demonstration. This document describes proposed capabilities, illustrative scenarios, and an intended direction—not claims that a fully autonomous system or operational blockchain has already been delivered.

01 / OVERVIEW

The idea in one minute

SOMAS is a proposed platform for creating AI organizations that work toward practical goals, rather than treating AI as one chat window or a collection of isolated tools.

The first idea: SOMAS Platform

A user describes an objective—such as running a restaurant, launching a store, maintaining a website, or coordinating household tasks. A Manager agent translates that objective into an organization of specialized agents. Those agents plan, execute, exchange findings, test results, and report progress. Human owners can examine decisions, costs, and evidence and control what agents are permitted to do.

The second idea: SOMAS Network

Independent SOMAS organizations could eventually connect through a network. Each organization retains its own goal and team while exchanging requests for expertise. A proposed verification layer would help record whether submitted work met agreed criteria, enabling auditable collaboration. Blockchain is a candidate for shared records where decentralization actually adds value; it is not necessary for every internal conversation.

A simple pictureThe Platform is how one organization is created and run. The Network is how many organizations could find one another, collaborate, and build shared trust.

02 / MOTIVATION

Why build SOMAS?

The ambition is to move from "AI helps me with a task" toward "AI helps organize and sustain an outcome."

The problem with isolated assistants

Most work is not a single prompt. Even a small project contains research, coordination, implementation, checking, communication, and revision. When each step lives in a different chat or application, people still act as the project manager: they remember the state, assign the work, reconcile contradictions, and decide what happens next.

What SOMAS would change

From conversation to organization: multiple specialists have explicit roles, shared objectives, and reporting relationships.

From one-time outputs to ongoing responsibility: the team can revisit its goal, inspect new evidence, and propose corrections.

From invisible automation to observable work: the owner can inspect who did what, why, at what cost, and with what result.

From identical agents to experience-based specialization: proposed trainable filters would adapt each agent’s decision preferences.

From isolated projects to an ecosystem: specialized SOMAS units could eventually request and verify services across a network.

The core promise

The desired result is not simply more AI-generated content. It is dependable coordination: tasks handed to the right specialist, results checked before they matter, learning measured rather than assumed, and humans retained as the authority for sensitive or consequential actions.

Important limitAn agent appearing to work in a 3D scene is not proof it can work autonomously. SOMAS should distinguish visual simulations, replayed traces, and actual tool-driven executions.

03 / PLATFORM CONCEPT

How the SOMAS Platform works

Think of SOMAS as a management layer over a collection of specialized AI workers, shared tools, and traceable workflows.

The journey from a goal to an operating team

StageWhat happensWhat the user sees
1. DescribeThe owner enters a mission, boundaries and available resources.A clear goal and the proposed scope.
2. OrganizeThe Manager proposes departments, roles, tasks and dependencies.An interactive team structure.
3. ConnectApproved tools, data and AI providers are assigned to roles.Connection and permission settings.
4. WorkAgents research, build, test, coordinate and report.Activity, artifacts, task status and cost.
5. EvaluateOutputs are checked against defined acceptance criteria.Evidence, test results and approvals.
6. ImproveUseful feedback influences future decisions and workflows.Learning history and measurable change.

What the human owner controls

The owner should decide the high-level goal, budgets, approved integrations, deployment permissions, approval thresholds, and whether the team may continue beyond a task. SOMAS may suggest improvements but should not silently spend money, deploy high-impact changes, access private data, or act in regulated contexts without the relevant authorization.

What makes a Manager different from a chatbot?

A Manager is responsible for interpreting the goal, selecting specialists, tracking dependencies, resolving conflicts, requesting approvals, reviewing evidence, and deciding which question or task should be handled next. A conventional assistant mostly responds to one prompt; a Manager maintains organizational context across a longer sequence of work.

Create your own Manager

A Sub-SOMAS is not a branch office operated by the Root Manager. It is a goal-driven organization established for a specific user or project.

Example: a founder wants an online store

The user signs up, selects "Make My Own Manager," and enters a goal: "Launch a small online store for handmade products, keep the initial budget low, and ask permission before publishing or purchasing anything."

Ownership principleEach user’s SOMAS has isolated data, permissions and resources. Root SOMAS manages the SOMAS service; a Sub-SOMAS Manager manages only its authorized workspace and goal.

06 / CONTINUOUS WORK

How agents collaborate and improve

An organization only becomes useful when independent specialists coordinate through a predictable cycle.

An example inside the team

The Virtual Customer notices that a checkout form confuses users. It opens an issue with screenshots and observations. The Manager asks the Designer to propose an improvement and the Engineer to implement it. The Reliability Engineer runs tests; the Virtual Customer repeats the journey. The Manager compares results, asks the human owner to approve deployment if required, and records what worked.

Concurrent work, not one agent at a time

Independent tasks should run in parallel when safe. A researcher might collect requirements while a designer explores alternatives and an engineer checks technical feasibility. Dependencies are still respected: deployment waits for tests and approvals. In a visual demo, multiple characters may animate simultaneously, but movements should correspond to task states and traces instead of arbitrary activity.

Continual does not mean uncontrolled

A SOMAS may repeatedly observe, propose, and evaluate work, but every loop operates under stopping rules: budget ceilings, rate limits, approvals, task relevance, timeout rules, and permission boundaries. Some tasks should end decisively rather than continuing forever.

07 / ADAPTIVE INTELLIGENCE

The proposed learning mechanism

SOMAS explores a form of per-agent specialization that sits alongside general-purpose language models.

Why a separate trainable filter?

A large language model can interpret requests and generate plans. But an organization also repeatedly makes small choices: which tool to use, which teammate to consult, how strongly to verify an answer, when to escalate, or which previous strategy deserves preference. A lightweight trainable component could influence these decisions without retraining the full language model.

Illustrative three-part policy

Policy dimensionExample of what it influences
Action selectionWhether to research, draft, test, ask or escalate.
Tool / strategy selectionWhich approved API, approach or workflow to use.
Coordination / verificationWho should review work and how much checking is needed.

What “learning value” should mean

The learning value should be a defined metric, not a decorative number that rises automatically. In a prototype, it might summarize recent evaluation performance against a known task set, using versioned scoring rules. A visible history should show the before/after score, the evaluated task type, sample size, confidence limits where relevant, and the change that was applied.

A concrete improvement example

Suppose an agent repeatedly chooses an expensive external search tool when an approved cached data source contains the answer. Evaluation detects higher cost with no improvement in quality. A trained selection policy shifts future comparable requests toward the cached source. The dashboard shows the change in tool preference alongside observed cost and accuracy outcomes. If evidence is insufficient, the filter should not claim to have learned.

Research direction, not proven breakthroughThis mechanism is a product hypothesis to test experimentally. Real learning needs measurable evaluations, reproducible training data, rollback ability and safeguards against reward hacking.

08 / SCENARIO STUDY

Example: managing a restaurant

A restaurant is an example of how many small, connected activities become one operating objective.

Owner’s goal

"Help improve service quality, reduce waste, manage reservations and understand weekly performance. Do not purchase ingredients or change prices without my approval."

TimePossible agent teamworkOwner-facing result

What makes this an organization

The agents share one objective and each other’s outputs. The inventory decision informs menu planning; customer feedback influences service improvements; cost analysis constrains the Manager’s proposed actions. The owner does not need to repeat all the context in separate chats.

Boundaries

SOMAS must not pretend that it operates a physical restaurant independently. Real reservations, purchasing and payment changes require trusted integrations, permissions, and human override. The demo may simulate these operations but should label them as such.

09 / SCENARIO STUDY

Example: launching an online store

The store owner begins with a goal and a few constraints—not a complete list of software tickets.

Week 1: structure the business

The Manager asks the Research Agent to clarify customer segments, the Designer to create a visual direction, and the Engineer to select a deployable architecture. A Finance Agent estimates recurring costs. The owner approves a coherent plan before any public launch.

Week 2: build and test

Engineering sets up a storefront and product catalog. Design reviews accessibility and checkout clarity. A Virtual Customer browses as a first-time visitor, attempts common shopping journeys, and records confusing steps. The Manager consolidates test outcomes into a launch checklist.

After launch: controlled evolution

The agents monitor authorized metrics, propose copy improvements, identify broken pages, and create testable changes. Rather than altering production at random, they prepare reviewable changes with evidence and rollback instructions. The human owner chooses whether changes go live.

Example of observable teamworkCustomer feedback -> Marketing hypothesis -> Design variation -> Engineering change -> Virtual Customer test -> Manager review -> Owner approval. Every arrow can be inspected as a recorded task handoff.

Potential value

The owner gains a coordinated project team, with specialist contributions and a single place to follow decisions. SOMAS is not promised to replace employees; it aims to reduce the coordination burden of repetitive digital work.

What changes across domains?

The organization chart, tool permissions, approval thresholds, evaluation methods and reporting rules. A household budget assistant should not have the same access or autonomy as a code-testing agent. High-stakes settings, especially healthcare and financial operations, require domain-specific oversight and restrictions.

One platform; many organizations

The long-term idea is not to hard-code six separate products. SOMAS would supply reusable organizational building blocks that can be configured for different goals, while acknowledging that domain-specific software and reliable data sources remain necessary.

11 / A NETWORK OF ORGANIZATIONS

The SOMAS Network idea

After users can create useful individual SOMAS organizations, a second opportunity emerges: letting those organizations cooperate across boundaries.

Root, Sub-SOMAS and specialized service providers

Root SOMAS supports the platform. User-created Sub-SOMAS units represent their own owners and objectives. Some Sub-SOMAS units might offer narrow expertise—such as code review, translation, design testing or data analysis—under explicit marketplace and privacy rules. The proposed network would help them discover partners and coordinate work.

What each SOMAS could publish

Public or discoverablePrivate by default
Network identity and declared capabilitiesPrivate prompts, personal information and credentials
Non-sensitive service descriptionsInternal agent messages and proprietary data
Opt-in quality or reputation summariesUnapproved artifacts and detailed business records
Verification references where permittedPayment, health or account information

Why use a network?

A restaurant SOMAS may need a website audit. A school SOMAS may need a specialist in accessibility. A research SOMAS may need independent validation of a dataset transformation. Instead of duplicating every specialist inside every organization, a network could route well-scoped requests to another organization that can fulfill them.

Blockchain remains a proposed componentA blockchain may offer tamper-evident shared records and independent verification, but only if the network’s consensus, incentives, and threat model justify it. The demo’s 10,000 example Sub-SOMAS entries are illustrative data—not 10,000 active network participants.

12 / PROPOSED NETWORK PROTOCOL

How verification might work

The hard part of a decentralized AI network is not drawing connections. It is deciding what counts as valid work and who is trusted to judge it.

Example: one SOMAS asks another for a security review

A Store SOMAS submits a tightly scoped request: review an open-source component for known vulnerabilities and produce a report in a defined format. A Security SOMAS accepts it and returns findings with version references and evidence. A separate verifier checks whether the report covers the required tests and whether cited evidence can be reproduced. The requesting owner can accept, reject or dispute the result.

What could be recorded?

A request identifier, timestamps, the parties’ network identities, an agreed task specification, cryptographic commitments or hashes for permitted artifacts, verifier decisions, and dispute outcomes. Sensitive source files should not be placed directly on a public ledger.

What cannot automatically be proven

A blockchain can establish that a record exists and has not been altered under a given consensus model; it cannot, by itself, establish that an AI-generated answer is correct. Evaluation quality, verifier independence, collusion resistance, and error resolution are separate design problems.

13 / MAKING PROGRESS MEASURABLE

Learning, identity and reputation

Every SOMAS should be understandable as an entity with a goal, an age, and a track record—but metrics must retain their meaning.

FieldMeaning in SOMASImportant qualification
Name / identifierHuman-readable identity plus a stable internal ID.Names are not proof of ownership.
GoalWhat the organization is authorized to pursue.A goal may be edited, paused or completed.
AgeElapsed time since its creation.Age is not time spent actively working.
Learning valueDefined evaluation-based progress measure.Must include scoring rules and history.
ActivityRecorded tasks, communication and approvals.Simulated vs real must be distinguished.
VerificationResults under an explicit review or protocol.Not equivalent to universal correctness.
CostTracked usage and estimated provider charges.Must state what costs are included.

A meaningful learning-history view

Instead of a decorative line that rises forever, show each evaluation milestone. The viewer can open an entry to see the task category, evaluation sample, prior score, new score, reason for the change, and whether it was rolled back. For the public network, expose only aggregates that are safe to share.

Reputation needs resistance to manipulation

A proposed marketplace would need protections against fake feedback, colluding SOMAS accounts, duplicate submissions, low-quality spam, and self-verified work. Reliability should depend on independently checkable performance rather than raw activity volume.

14 / MAKING INVISIBLE WORK VISIBLE

What visitors experience in the demo

The SOMAS demo is a storytelling interface for the future product: visitors can see a digital organization behave, while understanding what is simulated.

Two doors into the platform

The SOMAS Platform page introduces two choices: "Root Manager" and "Make My Own Manager." Root Manager opens an observer view of the platform team’s roles, work, learning history and status. Make My Own Manager helps a user describe a new goal, assemble a proposed team, assign tools and approvals, and create a personal workspace.

A visible, active organization

In the 3D headquarters, characters sit, walk, inspect work, report, learn, and collaborate. Multiple agents act concurrently rather than one character moving at a time. Each movement corresponds to a task state, event or replay marker. Clicking any character shows the name, responsibility, goal, age, current task, learning value and history, model provider and usage cost.

Network explorer

The network page shows Root SOMAS connected with a large example set of Sub-SOMAS units. A compact 3D visualization sits alongside blockchain-style charts, a searchable SOMAS table, activity timeline, verification records and detailed node inspection. The explorer should support approximately 10,000 example Sub-SOMAS records without trying to animate 10,000 detailed characters at once.

Community and participation

Visitors can discuss the concept, view videos, contact the team, book a meeting, or express interest as an investor, engineer or contributor. Authentication and approval flows support community participation and trusted management.

Demonstration integrityThe demo may contain 100,000 synthetic user profiles, 10,000 synthetic Sub-SOMAS units and example task histories. Those records must never be presented as real customers, real economic activity or a deployed blockchain.

15 / HUMAN AUTHORITY AND TRUST

Who controls the system?

Autonomous work should be delegated; responsibility should not disappear.

The permissions model

The owner authorizes a SOMAS workspace, its connected tools and its spending budget.

Agents use the least access necessary to perform each assigned job.

Sensitive decisions—payments, production deployments, public communications, access changes—can require explicit owner approval.

Action history records the actor, request, tool, result, review and timestamps.

Owners can pause a Manager, disable an integration, revoke keys and inspect or export their data.

User experience and community governance

Visitors can browse public concept material without granting sensitive access. Verified users can participate in discussion and request elevated community roles. Assistors and administrators may share operational tools, but only authorized administrators can approve or block Assistor accounts, and only the owner can approve new administrator authority. Account management must distinguish real people from synthetic exhibit records.

How the owner is contacted

The intended contact and meeting workflows route inquiries and role requests to the SOMAS owner. Meeting scheduling shows available time slots rather than exposing private calendar event details. Any Gmail or Calendar integration requires explicit authorization; entering an email address alone does not provide access.

What contributors could receive

Engineers, designers, researchers and community contributors may participate in technical work, testing, documentation and idea discussion. Depending on future funding and agreements, opportunities could include learning, portfolio experience, paid work or a negotiated role. No compensation should be promised before the terms, funding and deliverables are agreed.

Why cost transparency matters

An impressive AI team is not useful if its model calls cost more than the value of its work. SOMAS should make provider selection, per-agent token usage, estimated spending, task-level cost, approval thresholds and budget exhaustion easy to understand.

Open questions

How do we measure better agent decisions without rewarding misleading metrics?

What tasks should agents do independently, and which need a person in the loop?

How do we make several agents cooperate without wasting API calls or duplicating effort?

Does a decentralized protocol outperform a conventional service marketplace for specific kinds of work?

How do we preserve user privacy while supporting searchable network reputation?

The minimum proof of value

A small SOMAS team completes a concrete digital task end-to-end: clear objective, delegated work, visible tool use, independent checking, a measurable result, and transparent human approval. This matters more than inflated counts of animated agents.

18 / INVITATION

The long-term vision

SOMAS imagines a future where people create and guide organizations of AI specialists as naturally as they use software today.

A founder might delegate a product launch. A restaurant owner might supervise a digital operations team. A household might coordinate recurring tasks. A software organization might create specialized teams for coding, testing and customer feedback. Those teams could become better through measured experience, and—with the right safeguards—could collaborate with other SOMAS organizations through a wider network.

The goal is not to remove humans from responsibility. It is to make reliable intelligence organized, transparent, adaptable, and available to far more people.

Co-founders who want to turn the concept into a practical company.

AI and software engineers interested in orchestration, evaluation, memory and trainable policies.

Blockchain and security researchers interested in verification, reputation and incentives.
